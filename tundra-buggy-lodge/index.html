A hotel that drives out to the bears

The Lodge is a linked train of white units parked deep in the Churchill Wildlife Management Area, out at Polar Bear Point — well beyond the town, well beyond the road. You reach it by Tundra Buggy and you stay there. The bears come to you.

It sleeps up to 40 guests. Frontiers North runs it through the autumn, October into November, as the sea ice starts to form along Hudson Bay and the bears gather to wait for it. Aurora is possible from the rooftop deck, though less reliable than in deep winter.

Frontiers North is a Certified B Corporation, and the buggies are now electric — zero-emission, charged with renewable hydroelectric power.

Bunks on the tundra

Sleeping and washing

Accommodation is in two sleeper units, 20 guests each, in a shared bunk arrangement — closer to a sleeper car than a hotel room. Each berth has its own privacy curtain, window, and thermostat, so you control your own heat and your own view.

Showers and washrooms are shared. Pack accordingly, and pack light for the two nights — see the note about luggage below.

On the morning of Oct 5, mark your luggage and leave it by your lower bunk. Frontiers North moves it into town to the Tundra Inn while you're out on the buggy.

Better than you'd expect at 58° north

Meals

Chef-prepared, built around local and regional ingredients, served in a dining unit with panoramic windows — polar bears at the window during dinner is a normal occurrence rather than a brochure promise.

Dietary restrictions are accommodated with advance notice. This is the opposite of the train, which cannot accommodate them at all — so if you have restrictions, make sure Frontiers North knows well before October.

All Lodge meals are covered by the kitty fee, as are lunches out on the buggy.

The vehicle

The buggy days

The Tundra Buggy dates to 1979 and is the original purpose-built tundra vehicle. The current fleet is electric. It carries up to 40, though multi-day trips run 22 or fewer, and it has an elevated outdoor viewing deck at the back with a grated floor — which is where the photographs you've seen from these trips get taken from.

Excursions run a full day, roughly eight hours, departing straight from the Lodge. Lunch happens on board. Dress for standing still outdoors in Arctic wind rather than for walking around in it.
