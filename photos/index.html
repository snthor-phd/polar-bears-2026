The caravan album

Thirty-one days, twenty-some rigs and one very photogenic bear. Rather than everyone going home with a partial set, we're pooling them: add your photos to the shared album and you get everybody's. The Photo Team draws on it for the end-of-trip slideshow, and the Trip Journal pulls pictures from it too.

Adding photos from your phone

Tap “Add your photos”

The album opens in Google Photos — in the app if you have it, otherwise in your browser. First time only, you'll be asked to join the album.

Hit Add photos

Pick from your camera roll. Select as many as you like — whole days at a time is fine. Videos work too.

Wait for Wi‑Fi

Campground Wi‑Fi is thin and Churchill data is expensive. Queue them up and let them upload when you're on a good connection — it resumes on its own.

Nothing gets published anywhere else. The album is visible only to people with the link — the caravan and whoever you choose to share it with. Anyone can remove a photo they added, any time.

Shooting notes for this trip

Bears from the buggy

  • Longest lens you own, but don't ignore the wide shot — bear-in-landscape tells the story better than a headshot.
  • Shoot from the open window rather than through glass; heated buggy air over cold glass makes everything soft.
  • Fast shutter (1/1000 s or quicker) — the buggy rocks even when parked.
  • White bear on white snow fools the meter. Add about +1 stop of exposure compensation or the bear turns grey.

Aurora at night

  • Tripod, and a remote or the 2‑second timer.
  • Manual mode: widest aperture you have (f/2.8 or wider), ISO 1600–3200, 5–15 s. Shorter when the lights are moving fast.
  • Manual focus, set on a bright star, then tape the ring — autofocus hunts in the dark.
  • Recent phones do very well in night mode. Brace it on something solid and let it run the full exposure.
  • Shoot RAW if you can. Aurora colour is where phone processing struggles most.

Gear in the cold

  • Cold kills batteries. Carry two or three spares in an inside pocket and rotate them.
  • Coming back into a warm buggy or lodge fogs and condenses gear — bag the camera before you go in and let it warm up sealed.
  • Thin liner gloves so you can work the controls without going bare‑handed.

Make them findable

  • Upload as you go rather than in one heap at the end — the album stays roughly in date order.
  • Leave a short comment on the ones that need context: who, where, what happened next.
  • Got somebody's rig or a good portrait of them? That's the photo they don't have. Post it.
